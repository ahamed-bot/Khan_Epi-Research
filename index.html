RESEARCH

Plant epigenetics across evolutionary, molecular and genomic scales

My research focuses on understanding how chromatin regulatory systems evolve and how their molecular functions influence genome organisation, development and environmental adaptation in plants.

I combine evolutionary biology, molecular biology, chromatin biology, genomics and computational approaches to investigate the emergence, diversification and functional organisation of plant epigenetic regulatory networks.

RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

From evolutionary innovation to chromatin function

My research connects evolutionary history, molecular mechanisms and genome organisation to understand how plant epigenetic systems regulate biological processes.

01

CHROMATIN REGULATION • PLANT DEVELOPMENT

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programs.

Read the paper →
02

EVOLUTION • EPIGENETICS • CHROMATIN

Interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins in plant evolution

This work explores how Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins have evolved across plant lineages, highlighting the molecular and evolutionary relationships that shape chromatin regulatory systems.

The research provides an evolutionary framework for understanding how chromatin regulatory components emerge, diversify and cooperate to control plant development and epigenome organisation.

Read the paper →
03

PWO EVOLUTION • PRC2 • PLANT CHROMATIN

PWO proteins are associated with PRC2 since their emergence in vascular plants

This research investigates the evolutionary emergence of PWWP-domain interactor of Polycombs (PWO) proteins and their association with Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2) across plant evolution.

The findings provide insight into the ancient relationship between PWO proteins and PRC2 and reveal how this regulatory association has been maintained since the emergence of vascular plants.
