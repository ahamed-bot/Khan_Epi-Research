RESEARCHER IN PLANT EPIGENETICS

Understanding how epigenetic mechanisms shape plant development and adaptation.

My research investigates the evolution and function of chromatin regulatory systems, with a particular focus on Polycomb-associated proteins, chromatin organisation, genome regulation and plant developmental responses.

I combine molecular biology, evolutionary analysis, genomics and computational approaches to understand how regulatory systems emerge, diversify and function across plant lineages.

RESEARCH

From evolutionary history to molecular mechanism.

My research explores how chromatin regulatory systems evolved and how their molecular interactions influence genome organisation, development and environmental responses in plants.

01

Evolution of Chromatin Regulation

Reconstructing the emergence and diversification of chromatin regulators across the green lineage and broader eukaryotic evolution.

02

Molecular Function

Understanding how chromatin proteins interact with one another, DNA and chromatin to establish regulatory states.

03

Genome Organisation

Investigating how chromatin regulatory networks influence genome architecture, nuclear organisation and gene regulation.

04

Development & Adaptation

Exploring how epigenetic regulation contributes to developmental programmes, cellular identity and plant responses to environmental conditions.

01 / CHROMATIN REGULATION

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation in Arabidopsis

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programs.

Khan et al., 2026 bioRxiv
Read the publication →
02 / EVOLUTION & EPIGENETICS

Interplay between Polycomb-group histone modifiers and accessory proteins in plant evolution

This work explores the evolutionary interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins, highlighting how regulatory partnerships have contributed to the diversification of plant chromatin regulation.

Khan, Ghosh & Schubert, 2025 Current Opinion in Plant Biology
