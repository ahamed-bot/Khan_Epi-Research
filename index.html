RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

From molecular interactions to plant development

Current work focuses on the molecular and evolutionary relationships between chromatin regulators and their consequences for plant development.

2026 • CURRENT RESEARCH

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation to prevent premature differentiation and ectopic lignin deposition in Arabidopsis

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organisation and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programmes.

Read paper / DOI →
2025 • REVIEW

Interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins in plant evolution

This work examines how Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and their accessory proteins have evolved and diversified across plant lineages, highlighting the molecular relationships that shape epigenetic regulation.

The review provides an evolutionary framework for understanding how chromatin regulatory systems have been assembled, modified and functionally integrated throughout plant evolution.

Read paper / DOI →
2026 • PLANT EVOLUTION

PWO proteins are associated with PRC2 since their emergence in vascular plants

This research investigates the evolutionary emergence of PWO proteins and their association with Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 across plant evolution.

The study provides insight into how PWO proteins became integrated into plant chromatin regulatory systems and how their relationships with PRC2 have been maintained throughout vascular plant evolution.

ABOUT

Understanding plant epigenome regulation through evolution and function

My research combines molecular biology, chromatin biology, evolutionary analysis and computational approaches to investigate how plant epigenetic regulatory systems are constructed and how they function across biological contexts.

A central goal is to connect the evolution of chromatin regulators with their molecular function and their consequences for genome organisation, development and plant adaptation.
