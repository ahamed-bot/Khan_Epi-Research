RESEARCH

Plant epigenetics at the intersection of evolution, chromatin and genome organisation.

My research focuses on understanding how plant chromatin regulatory systems evolve, function and influence genome organisation, developmental programmes and adaptation.

I combine molecular biology, chromatin biology, evolutionary analysis, genomics and computational approaches to understand how regulatory mechanisms are established and diversified across plant lineages.

RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

From molecular interactions to plant developmental regulation

My research explores how chromatin-associated proteins work together to establish epigenetic states and regulate genome function during plant development.

RESEARCH 01

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programmes.

Evolutionary framework connecting Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins across plant lineages.

RESEARCH 02

Evolution of plant epigenetic regulation

The research programme investigates how Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins have evolved across plant lineages and how their interactions contribute to epigenetic regulation.

By connecting evolutionary diversification with molecular function, this work aims to understand how complex chromatin regulatory systems emerged and became integrated into plant developmental programmes.
