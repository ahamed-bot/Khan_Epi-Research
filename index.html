RESEARCH INTEREST

Understanding plant epigenetic mechanisms across evolutionary scales.

My research focuses on the evolution and function of plant chromatin regulatory networks and their contribution to genome organisation and developmental regulation.

I combine molecular biology, chromatin biology, evolutionary analysis, genomics and computational approaches to understand how epigenetic regulatory systems emerge, diversify and function across plants.

RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

From chromatin regulation to plant development and evolution

My research connects molecular mechanisms of chromatin regulation with genome organisation, plant development and the evolutionary diversification of epigenetic systems.

RESEARCH 01

PWO1–TRB coordination in chromatin regulation

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programs.

RESEARCH 02

Evolution of plant epigenetic regulation

This research explores how Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins have evolved across plant lineages.

By integrating evolutionary history with molecular function, the work provides a framework for understanding how chromatin regulatory systems emerge, diversify and acquire new biological functions during plant evolution.
