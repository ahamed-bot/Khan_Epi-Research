RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

Connecting chromatin regulation, evolution and plant development

My research investigates how chromatin regulatory proteins and their evolutionary diversification influence genome organisation and developmental programmes in plants.

01 / CURRENT RESEARCH

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation to prevent premature differentiation and ectopic lignin deposition in Arabidopsis

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programs.

Read the paper / bioRxiv →
02 / EVOLUTIONARY EPIGENETICS

Interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins in plant evolution

This work explores the evolutionary interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins, focusing on how chromatin regulatory systems have emerged, diversified and become functionally integrated throughout plant evolution.

Read publication →
03 / PWO EVOLUTION

PWO proteins are associated with PRC2 since their emergence in vascular plants

This research investigates the evolutionary emergence of PWO proteins and their association with Polycomb Repressive Complex 2 (PRC2), revealing how these chromatin-associated proteins have been integrated into regulatory systems during plant evolution.
