RESEARCH

Understanding plant epigenetic mechanisms across evolutionary and functional scales.

My research combines molecular biology, chromatin biology, evolutionary analysis, genomics and computational approaches to investigate the evolution and function of plant epigenetic regulatory systems.

A particular focus is placed on Polycomb-associated proteins, chromatin regulatory networks, nuclear organisation and the molecular mechanisms connecting chromatin states with plant development and adaptation.

RESEARCH FRAMEWORK

From chromatin regulation to plant development

My research explores how molecular interactions between chromatin regulators influence epigenetic states, genome organisation and developmental programmes.

RESEARCH 01

PWO1–TRB coordination in chromatin regulation

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organisation and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programmes.

Read the publication →

RESEARCH 02

Evolution of Polycomb-associated chromatin regulatory systems

This research explores the evolutionary interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins across plant lineages.

By integrating evolutionary history with molecular function, this work provides a framework for understanding how epigenetic regulatory networks emerge, diversify and acquire new functions during plant evolution.
