THE CENTRAL QUESTION

How do plant chromatin regulatory systems evolve, function and shape genome organisation?

The Khan Research Lab explores the molecular and evolutionary principles that connect chromatin regulation with genome organisation and plant adaptation.

Our research brings together evolutionary biology, molecular biology, genomics, epigenetics, chromatin biology and computational approaches to understand how regulatory systems emerge, diversify and function across plants.

RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

Five interconnected objectives

01

Reconstruct evolutionary histories

Determine how plant chromatin regulatory systems emerged, diversified and were remodelled throughout plant evolution.

02

Define molecular mechanisms

Identify the molecular interactions that recruit and position chromatin regulatory machinery at specific genomic regions.

03

Connect chromatin to genome architecture

Investigate how chromatin regulatory networks influence three-dimensional genome organisation and nuclear compartmentalisation.

04

Understand developmental regulation

Determine how epigenome regulation contributes to developmental programmes and cellular identity.

05

Explain adaptation

Establish how changes in chromatin state and genome organisation contribute to responses to environmental conditions.

ABOUT THE LAB

Building a research programme around plant epigenome regulation

The Khan Research Lab is envisioned as an interdisciplinary research programme investigating the evolution, molecular function and biological consequences of plant chromatin regulatory networks.

By connecting evolutionary biology with molecular mechanisms, genome organisation and plant adaptation, the lab seeks to understand how regulatory systems are built, modified and deployed across biological contexts.
