RESEARCH INTEREST

Understanding plant epigenetic regulation across evolutionary and molecular scales.

My research focuses on understanding how chromatin regulatory systems evolve and how their molecular interactions influence genome organisation, developmental programmes and plant adaptation.

I combine evolutionary biology, molecular biology, chromatin biology, genomics and computational approaches to investigate the mechanisms that establish and regulate epigenetic states in plants.

FEATURED RESEARCH • 01

PWO1 and TRB proteins coordinate chromatin regulation

This study shows that PWO1 and TRB proteins co-occupy telomeres, including interspersed telo-box motifs, to regulate chromatin organization and plant development, particularly ectopic lignin deposition.

Our findings reveal how these nuclear protein factors coordinate epigenetic states in Arabidopsis thaliana, providing a framework for understanding the control of developmental programs.

Read the preprint ↗

FEATURED REVIEW • 02

Interplay between Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and accessory proteins in plant evolution

This work explores how Polycomb-group associated histone modifiers and their accessory proteins have evolved across plant lineages and how their interactions contribute to epigenetic regulation.

The review provides an evolutionary perspective on the emergence, diversification and functional interplay of chromatin regulatory systems in plants.
